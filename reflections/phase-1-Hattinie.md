# Phase 1 reflection — Hattinie

## What did I do?

During Phase 1, I served as the Build Lead for our project. My responsibilities included setting up my working environment, verifying parts of the toolchain, and contributing to project documentation. I worked on the Business Profile and Charter while also conducting research into the microlending industry to better understand the business context of Thusanang Microlending.
I also worked closely with my teammate throughout the phase. Although we often worked on documents independently before combining our work, regular discussions helped us compare ideas and improve the final versions of the artefacts.

## What did I find hard?

The most difficult part of Phase 1 was becoming comfortable with Git, GitHub, and the associated command-line workflow. Understanding how to commit changes, push updates, work with branches, and write meaningful commit messages took time and required repeated practice.
I also found it challenging to follow and interpret all the project instructions correctly. There were many documents, tools, and requirements to understand, and I sometimes found it difficult to determine exactly what was expected.
Another challenge was working with the different text-based formats and tools required for the project. Learning how these tools fit together and how they should be used within the repository took longer than I expected.

## What would I do differently?

If I were to start Phase 1 again, I would spend more time becoming familiar with the tools and commands before beginning the project work itself. A stronger understanding of Git, GitHub, and the supporting tools would have made many tasks easier and reduced uncertainty.
I would also review files and documentation more carefully before committing changes. In addition, I would continue the practice of having my work reviewed by my teammate before finalising it, as I found that collaboration often helped identify mistakes and improve the overall quality of the work.

## What do I understand now that I did not before?

One of the most important lessons I learned is how much regular communication and collaboration improve both productivity and understanding. Discussing ideas and challenges with my teammate often helped clarify requirements and provided different perspectives on the same problem.
I also have a better understanding of how version control supports a project and why careful documentation is important. Finally, I learned that asking questions and requesting assistance when needed is an important part of the learning process. Rather than struggling alone for too long, it is often more effective to seek guidance and then continue working with a better understanding of the task.
